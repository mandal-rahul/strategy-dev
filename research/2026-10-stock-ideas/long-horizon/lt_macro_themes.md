## 1. Bottom line (for 2–3-year holdings)

1. **US index: low expected excess return.** The S&P 500 is at ~18.7x consensus CY27 EPS (calc). The 10-year Treasury yields 5.24% [team]. A published regression of forward P/E against 3-year returns implies **~7%/yr** at 19x, with weak fit (R² 0.22) (S8, Oct 2024, M). My scenario-weighted estimate is **~4%/yr in USD** (calc: 0.5×5.5 + 0.2×14.4 + 0.3×(−6)). That is roughly what a 10-year Treasury bought today locks in. **Own specific, reasonably priced US businesses, not the index or the AI-capex chain.** The "other 493" trade at ~17.3x [team].
2. **AI capex: the growth rate falls in 2028.** Goldman sees hyperscaler capex at ~$1.2T in 2027 (+54%), then $1.4T in 2028 (**+12%**) (S19, Oct 2026, M). BofA sees hyperscaler FCF at **−$144B in 2027 and −$186B in 2028** (S19, M). Wells Fargo expects the market to start treating **2027 as a potential peak year** (S19, Oct 7 2026, M). Over a 2–3-year hold, the first and second derivatives point in different directions.
3. **India: better expected return, but a real bear analogue.** The Nifty is at ~17x blended forward and 15.8x FY28 consensus (calc). The long-period average is ~20.5x [team]. **Base case ~11–12%/yr in INR.** The closest bad analogue is **2010–13** (memory): inflation, RBI hikes, a rupee crisis and fiscal slippage left the Nifty **flat in INR and down ~30% in USD over three years**. Today has all four ingredients.
4. **Rupee: expect slower depreciation from here.** The 20-year depreciation rate is ~4%/yr (calc, memory endpoints). Forecasts are milder: **97 by Mar-2027 and 99 by Mar-2028** (BMI [team]; Ashika S16, M). My base is **~100–103 by end-2029** (≈1–2%/yr). A peace deal could take it back to ~92–93 [team]. The global sleeve's currency tailwind is real, but smaller than history.
5. **Theme ranking (best first):** India private and PSU banks → India financialisation (insurers first) → power demand via cheaply valued generators and utilities → healthcare (managed care, medtech) → foundry semis (TSMC, sized small) → India consumption (autos over staples) → India IT (cheap, payoff late) → India electronics/China+1 → India infrastructure and capex equipment → AI-capex hardware chain and memory → defence → oil. Full table in §6.

## 5. Structural themes, 2027–2029: evidence

### 5.1 AI capex, monetisation and the 2027–28 digestion risk
- **Capex path:** Goldman sees the top-5 at **~$1.2T in 2027 (+54%) and $1.4T in 2028 (+12%)**. S&P Global sees six companies at **$1.3T in 2027 vs ~$870B in 2026**. UBS has **$1.62T by 2028**. Jefferies cites a **~$2T backlog** as evidence of ROI. (S19, Apr–Oct 2026, M)
- **Definitions conflict:** the team file has Big-4 2026 at ~$710–750B and 2027 Street at ~$935B, with Evercore/BofA above $1T [team]. The gap comes from which companies are counted (4, 5 or 6) and lease treatment.
- **Cash:** BofA has aggregate hyperscaler FCF at **−$64B (2026), −$144B (2027), −$186B (2028)**. S&P Global shows **only Microsoft FCF-positive in 2027** among the six. (S19, Oct 2026, M)
- **ROI gap:** Goldman says the group needs **~$300B of annual AI revenue to break even**, and customers would need to spend **~$1T/yr on AI applications** for attractive returns. Lab run-rates today: OpenAI ~$50B (FT) / ≥$70B year-end target; Anthropic >$65B (July) [team]. (S19, M)
- **Market timing view:** Wells Fargo (Kwon): "the market will start thinking about a **potential peak year in 2027** soon, likely after 3Q EPS and midterms." Most investors expect capex growth to slow meaningfully in 2028. Pantheon expects moderation in 2027. (S19, Fortune Oct 7 2026, M)
- **Constraints:** power, labour, memory supply and siting (S19, M). About 20% of planned data-centre projects risk grid delays (IEA, S20, M).
- **Durability verdict:** demand for compute is real and contracted into 2027. **2028 is the risk year:** growth drops to roughly +12% or turns negative if financing tightens (debt funds ~35% of 2027 capex [team]). Equities in the capex chain usually peak when capex *growth* peaks, not when the level does (memory, a semis-cycle regularity).
- **For 2–3-year holders, prefer:** AI **users and monetisers** with positive FCF, which are de-rated (software), plus foundry. **Avoid:** levered builders (Oracle-type, neoclouds) and peak-margin memory.

### 5.2 Power, grid and data-centre electricity
- **Demand:**
  - IEA base case: global data-centre power **485 TWh (2025) → ~945 TWh (2030)**; US **224 → 426 TWh**, about half of US demand growth (S20, IEA Apr 2025 via S&P Global, M).
  - ICF: US electricity demand **+21% by 2030 and +39% by 2035** from 2026; only ~26GW of spare capacity above reliability needs (S20, M).
  - Goldman: US data-centre load of 31GW (2025), 41GW (2026), **66GW (2027)** [team].
- **Supply chain:** GE Vernova's gas-turbine backlog plus reservations is 116GW, with **slots into 2031** [team]. New 2026 orders are priced **10–20% above late 2025** (S21, M).
- **Pricing of equipment makers:** GE Vernova trades at **~38–42x forward** (Yahoo; other sources 33–63x) (S21, Oct 2026, L/M). One undated comparison puts ABB ~23x, Schneider ~17x and Siemens Energy ~16x on an unclear measure (S21, **L**). India capital goods trade at **48–115x** [team].
- **Pricing of generators and utilities:** **US utilities −8% YTD** on rates [team]. **NTPC ~10.9x vs ~13.5x 5-year average** [team].
- **India demand:**
  - CEA: peak demand **~277–289GW (FY27) → 335GW (FY30) → 366GW (FY32)**. A newer plan has **459GW by FY36** (S22, M).
  - Data centres **1.2GW (2025) → ~10GW (2030)**.
  - The new National Electricity Plan (FY28–37), including AI load, is due **by Mar 2027** (S22, M).
- **Verdict:** the most durable theme on this list. Power demand does not need AI ROI to work, because electrification, cooling and industry add to it. **The equipment makers are priced for it; regulated and contracted generators are not.** Own the cheap end. A 10-year of ~5% caps utility multiples but not their earnings.

### 5.3 Semiconductors
- **WSTS Spring 2026:** market **+~90% to ~$1.51T in 2026**, with **memory +~250% to >$800B** (calc: ~53% of the market). **2027 +~27% to ~$1.9T** (memory +32%, logic +27%) (S23, May 2026, H/M).
- **Memory cycle:**
  - Bloomberg Intelligence (Shuli Ren): shortage peaks Q2 2026, stabilises Q1 2027, **possible overcapacity by 2028** (S24, L/M).
  - UBS: shortage not easing **until mid-2028** at the earliest. SK hynix: 2027 may be the tightest year (S24, Jul 2026, M).
  - New supply: Micron's Idaho wafers **mid-2027**, a second fab **late 2028**; SK hynix's Yongin cleanroom **2027**; volume from the big new fabs **2028–29** (S24, M).
  - Micron gross margin is 87% and Samsung's OP margin 55% [team]. That is late-cycle.
- **Verdict:** foundry and leading-edge logic (TSMC) have the most durable position. They are fairly priced at ~20x on near-peak margins [team], so **hold with size discipline and add in the 2027–28 digestion**. **Memory is a 2026–27 trade, not a 2–3-year hold:** new supply lands in 2028 exactly as capex growth slows.

### 5.4 Healthcare (GLP-1, ageing, US policy)
- **GLP-1 market size by 2030:** Goldman **$95B** (cut from $130B; US peak $70B); JPM **$200B** (incretins); Gabelli ~$80B. JPM sees **~25M Americans on GLP-1s by 2030 vs ~10M in 2025** (S26, dates vary 2025–26, M). Volume is durable; price is falling.
- **Price compression:**
  - Medicare price **$245/month, $50 copay**; a bridge programme runs to **Dec 2027**. The Part D BALANCE pilot missed its 80% sign-up threshold (S26, M).
  - MFN models: **GLOBE (Part B, from Q4 2026)** and **GUARD (Part D, from Q1 2027)**, modelled at 16–18% savings.
  - IRA negotiation is moving discounts from ≤10% to **≥35%**. A bill (H.R. 7837) would start a 5-year MFN model on **1 Jan 2029**; it is only introduced (S27, Feb–Sep 2026, M).
  - 100% tariff on patented drugs; **0% on generics** [team].
- **Pricing:** managed care is de-rated (Elevance ~13x 2027 target EPS [team]). Novo is de-rated [team]. Lilly's valuation was not checked.
- **Verdict:** ageing demand is durable. **Pharma pricing power is structurally lower** through 2029, whoever wins in 2028, because drug-price pressure is bipartisan (memory/inference). Prefer **managed care recovering from a cost-trend shock, medtech and services** over branded-pharma pure plays. Indian generics exporters benefit from the 0% generic tariff and the weak rupee (inference).

### 5.5 Energy: oil after the Iran war vs. the transition
- **Supply outlook:**
  - EIA (Sep STEO): Middle East crude output stays **below pre-war until Q2 2027**; ~**600k bpd offline through end-2027**; a **UAE bypass pipeline in mid-2027** (S25, Sep 2026, M).
  - EIA July STEO: Brent **~$65 in 2027**, published *before* the 8-Jul ceasefire collapse, so stale.
  - Reuters poll (June): Brent **in the $70s by mid-2027**. Flows need only **60–70% of pre-war** to restore the pre-war oversupply outlook (S25, M).
  - Goldman's prolonged-closure case: **$115–120** (S25, spring 2026, M).
- **Now:** Brent ~$102; SPR at 1983 lows; refined products through Hormuz ~19% of pre-war [team].
- **Verdict:**
  - Oil equities are **a hedge, not a 3-year theme**. The structural path after the war is back toward a $65–80 surplus market; SPR refill demand is a partial offset.
  - Integrateds priced for $100 oil were already rejected [team]. TotalEnergies (~12–13x at $72) passes as a hedge, but **trim it after a ceasefire.**
  - **Transition:** energy-security spending after Hormuz supports grids, storage and nuclear (inference), but a 5% 10-year and a 7.3% G-sec hurt renewable-developer IRRs [team]. Express it through grid and utility earnings (§5.2).

### 5.6 Defence (Europe, India)
- **Europe:**
  - NATO target **5% of GDP by 2035** (3.5% core + 1.5% related), with a **2029 mid-course review**; ≥20% of budgets for equipment and R&D.
  - Europe plus Canada spent **$571B (2025) → $634B (2026), +11%**, slower than 2025's ~+19%.
  - **UK, France and Italy are struggling** to fund it. Germany plans >€200B by 2030 or "5% by 2029" (sources conflict). (S28, Jul–Sep 2026, M)
- **India:** MoD ₹7.84–7.85 lakh cr in FY27 (+~15%); **capital outlay ~₹2.19 lakh cr (+21.8%)**; 75% of procurement domestic; exports ₹38,424 cr in FY26 (+63%) (S30, Feb–Jul 2026, M/L). HAL order book ~₹2.54 lakh cr; BEL ₹73,882 cr (S30, L).
- **Pricing:** **BEL ~41x forward** (from 19.7x); Nifty Defence +18% over one year even in a falling market [team]. Rheinmetall forward P/E quoted at **19x, 32x and 61x by different sources**, trailing ~93x (S29, Aug–Sep 2026, **L, conflicting**).
- **Verdict:** order books are the most visible on this list. **Valuations already capitalise ~2030 earnings.** Peace (Ukraine or Iran) and budget strain in Europe's big-3 are de-rating risks. **No new money at current multiples.** Revisit on a 25–30% de-rating.

### 5.7 India financialisation (SIPs, insurance, wealth)
- **Household savings shift:**
  - Equities plus MFs rose from **~2% (FY12) to 15.2% (FY25)** of annual household financial savings; deposits fell from 40.9% (FY21) to 35.2% (FY25) (S31, Economic Survey via Finnovate, May 2026, M).
  - Equity and MF flows equalled **₹45 per ₹100 of deposit flows in FY25** (₹21 in FY24) (S31, M).
- **AUM outlook:** ~₹65 lakh cr (FY26) → **₹130 lakh cr (Bajaj AMC) to ₹150 lakh cr (Crisil) by FY30** (S31, Sep 2026, M).
- **Flows and headroom:** SIPs at a record **₹32,297 cr/month** (Aug 2026, +14% y/y) [team]. Only **~7% of households** hold MFs; MF AUM is ~33% of bank deposits (S31, M).
- **Pricing:** SBI Life at **~1.8x FY27 P/EV vs 3.0x FY25A**, with EV +15% and VNB +29% [team]. AMC, exchange and depository valuations were not checked.
- **Risks:** the cycle is tied to market returns. A bear-case drawdown would slow SIPs; SIPs persisted through the 2026 drawdown [team]. Also regulatory caps (TER, F&O curbs; memory) and GST/surrender-value rules for insurers.
- **Verdict:** high structural durability over 2027–29. **Insurers are the cheap way in today.** Capital-market intermediaries need a valuation check before buying.

### 5.8 India manufacturing, China+1, PLI and electronics
- **Exports:** smartphone exports **$9.84B in Q1 FY27 (+23.4%)**, 64.8% of $15.2B electronics exports. **FY27 ~$35B vs $29B in FY26** (industry estimate). **The US took 71%** (Apr–May). Global smartphone shipments **−13% y/y** in Q2 (S32, Jul 29 2026, M).
- **PLI:** cumulative iPhone exports passed **$50B by Dec 2025**. The smartphone PLI ended for Apple in **Mar 2026**, and industry wants more support (S32, M).
- **Tariff position:** the US rate on India is ~10% under Section 301, and **smartphones, petroleum and medicines (~40% of US-bound exports) are exempt**. August merchandise exports were **+26% y/y** [team]. The rupee near 97 helps.
- **Risks:** concentration in Apple and the US; litigation over the Section 301 tariffs; a narrow 25% Section 232 tariff on chips that could widen [team].
- **Verdict:** real and durable at the macro level, but **assembly margins are thin and listed proxies were not valued** (likely rich, memory). Stay neutral. Prefer exposure through banks financing the build-out and through exporters with pricing power.

### 5.9 India infrastructure and private-capex revival
- **Public spend:** central capex **+18% (Apr–Aug)**, but net tax is only +3.5%, making **H2 FY27 cuts likely** [team].
- **Private intentions:** RBI counts **₹5.6 lakh cr across 1,839 projects in FY26 (+12%)**, 89% greenfield. Funded capex is envisaged at **₹3.2 lakh cr in FY27 vs ₹2.6 lakh cr** (S33, Sep 29 2026, M).
- **Conversion is weak:** NIPFP finds private investment **fell to 10.3% of GDP in FY25 from 10.9% in FY23**, even as announcements more than doubled to ₹104 lakh cr (FY24–26). OBICUS capacity use is ~75%, range-bound. **Adani plus Reliance were ~28% of FY26 private capex** (S33, Aug–Sep 2026, M).
- **Pricing:** capital goods **48–115x**; L&T ~30.7x vs 34.4x 5-year median [team].
- **Verdict:** the cycle is real, but narrow and state-led. Valuations price a broad private upcycle that the data does not yet show. **Own lenders (SBI) and L&T at below-median multiples; avoid 50x+ equipment names.** The upside trigger is OBICUS above ~78% plus rate cuts in 2028 (inference).

### 5.10 India consumption premiumisation
- **Evidence:** premium brands are ~15% of FMCG volumes. **Rural share of super-premium volume rose from 30% (2021) to 42% (2025)**. Metro spend is shifting to housing, travel and phones (S35, 2025–26, M/L).
- **Cyclical headwinds for 2027:** monsoon **−12.6%** (worst since 2015), El Niño persisting, CPI heading to 6%, rate hikes [team].
- **Pricing:** HUL ~44x at a 52-week low; Maruti ~25x on trough margins [team].
- **Verdict:** structurally durable, but FY27–28 is a soft patch. **Staples are still expensive. Autos and discretionary carry peace-deal and rate-cut optionality** and are cheaper. Neutral overall; selective in autos.

### 5.11 India banking credit cycle
- **Credit vs deposits:** credit **~20% y/y** (Bernstein: ~18% underlying after a reporting change); **loan-to-deposit ratio near a decade high**; deposits lag (S34, mid-2026, M). System deposits ~17%, boosted by FCNR [team].
- **Outlook:** India Ratings expects growth to moderate in H2 FY27 (sector outlook neutral). CareEdge: **13–14.5% in FY27**, deposits lagging. S&P: ~12.5% for FY27–28 (Oct 2025, stale). Moody's: stable outlook for 12–18 months (S34, M).
- **Profit cycle:** BFSI led the profit/GDP high (S15, M). Kotak sees Q2 private-bank earnings +~20% and PSU banks flat [team].
- **Pricing:** ICICI ~2.8–2.9x book; **HDFC Bank ~1.8x vs ~2.7x 5-year average**; SBI ~11x P/E [team]. HDFC Bank's new CEO serves Oct 27 2026 – Oct 26 2029 [team].
- **Verdict:** a mid-teens loan growth runway with reasonable prices. **Credit growing faster than deposits at a decade-high LDR is a late-cycle marker**, so favour deposit-rich franchises and avoid wholesale-funded and unsecured-heavy lenders. Expect a margin squeeze in FY27, then relief as the RBI eases in 2028 (inference from S&P's CPI path).

### 5.12 India IT services
- **Deflation:** Kotak sees **3–3.5%/yr AI deflation through FY28**, with the crossover **around FY29**. Jefferies has ~**6% CAGR FY26–28**. CLSA expects AI volumes to exceed deflation **by FY30**, with mid-to-high single-digit USD growth by FY31. Sharekhan notes that 3–5-year contracts back-end the pain to FY28 and beyond (S36, Mar–Aug 2026, M).
- **Pricing:** ~14–15x forward vs ~26x 5-year average [team].
- **Verdict:** cheap, with a dividend floor and a rupee hedge, but the earnings inflection arrives **at the very end of the horizon**. Keep small, as a hedge that is already sized in the near-term plan [team].

## 9. Implications for picking 2–3-year holdings

1. **Screen out businesses whose 2029 earnings need AI capex growth to continue past 2027.** Memory, neoclouds, levered builders and 40x+ equipment fall here. Foundry is the exception, but size it small.
2. **Valuation discipline matters more over 3 years than over 3 months.** With the US ERP ≈ 0 and India EPS at a record profit/GDP share, prefer names at or below their own 5-year median multiple. Every theme on the "avoid" list fails that test, not the durability test.
3. **India gets the larger equity weight (expected ~10% vs ~6% INR).** Keep a US sleeve for stock-specific mispricing (de-rated software, managed care, quality compounders in the 493) and as an oil, monsoon and rupee hedge. **Do not count on 4%/yr rupee depreciation; budget 1–2%.**
4. **Read-across to the current near-term list** (FINAL_RECOMMENDATIONS.md):
   - **Fits 2–3-year themes:** ICICI, HDFC Bank and SBI (#1); NTPC (#3); SBI Life (#2, currently optional); Elevance (#4); TSMC (#5); Maruti (#6).
   - **Hedge, not core:** TotalEnergies (trim after a ceasefire); TCS and Infosys (#7, payoff late); L&T (#9, acceptable only below its median multiple).
   - **Thesis outside these themes, judge on stock merits:** Intuit, Adobe, Uber (AV checkpoint Jan 2028), Netflix, Reliance.
5. **Re-underwrite checkpoints:**
   - Hyperscaler 2027 capex guides (Oct 2026–Feb 2027).
   - India Budget (1 Feb 2027).
   - US–China truce (10 Jan 2027).
   - Memory pricing in mid-2027.
