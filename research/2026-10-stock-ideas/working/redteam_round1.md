## 1. Global picks: evidence

### G1. Uber: CONDITIONAL
**What the draft got wrong or left out**
1. **Delivery Hero is a signed deal, not an "unverified" rumour.** Uber agreed on **Jul 16, 2026** to buy Delivery Hero: €41.50/share in cash, about $14.8B equity value, closing expected **H2 2027**. Uber already owned about 25%, and Prosus agreed to tender about 17%. Sources: [Irish Times, 2026-07-16](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/07/16/uber-agrees-148bn-deal-to-buy-delivery-hero/); [Quartz, 2026-07-16](https://qz.com/uber-acquisition-delivery-hero-14-billion-071626).
   - Financing: a committed bridge of about €14B, new debt and term loans (which also refinance Delivery Hero's debt), and a new $7.7B revolver. Uber targets gross leverage below 2x ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/143838-uber-lines-up-7-7b-revolver-to-fund-delivery-hero-takeover/)).
   - **Implication:** for the next 2–3 years, Uber's FCF will go to M&A, debt service and the >$10B robotaxi plan, not to buybacks. The draft's FCF-yield argument ignores this. Integration and antitrust risk run across ~99 markets.
2. **Waymo has confirmed it will leave; this is not "reports it may".** Around Jul 24, 2026, Waymo told Uber it will launch its own app in **Austin and Atlanta in January 2028**. Exclusivity ends then, the current contract runs to May 2028, and Phoenix had already ended in May. The FT reported that Waymo was weighing a full exit. Sources: [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/waymo-uber-exclusivity-ends-austin-atlanta-robotaxi); [Automotive World](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/uber-confirms-end-of-tie-up-with-waymo-as-rivalry-heats-up/). The draft's "what would prove us wrong" includes "Waymo goes direct-to-consumer in Uber's top cities". **That is already announced.**
3. **Stock comp.** "TTM FCF >$10B, ~7% yield" is before stock-based compensation. Uber's SBC is about $1.8–2.0B a year (mem). The SBC-adjusted FCF yield is about 5.6–5.8% (calc). That is still fine, but not 7%.
4. **Results date conflict.** One aggregator gives Q3 on **Nov 2**, not Nov 4 ([Bullstory](https://bullstory.io/en/stocks/uber/earnings/2026-q3)). Confirm it.
5. **The "Uber-2023" analogy fails.** In 2023 Uber was crossing into GAAP operating profit, with S&P inclusion as a catalyst. Today the debate is about its terminal value against AV fleets, and the decisive dates (2028) lie beyond any 12-month catalyst. It is cheap on cash flow but slow to prove out, which makes it a "show-me" stock rather than an inflection.
6. **Interaction with GOOGL.** Alphabet owns Waymo, the main threat to Uber. If GOOGL stays in the portfolio, call the pair a deliberate hedge.

**Verdict:** keep at about 10%, conviction 3/5. Buying now is acceptable (results are at least 3 weeks away).

### G3. Netflix: CONDITIONAL
1. **FCF is flattered by a one-off.** Netflix received a **$2.8B Warner Bros. termination fee in Q1 2026**. The company raised its 2026 FCF guide **from $11B to ~$12.5B "due primarily to the after-tax impact" of that fee**. Source: [Netflix Q1-26 letter, SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000137/ex991_q126.htm).
   - Underlying P/FCF ≈ $308B / $11B ≈ **28x (calc)**, not 24.7x.
   - GuruFocus's TTM P/FCF of 26.7 also includes the fee, because Q1 FCF was $5.1B.
2. **EPS is also flattered.** Q1 diluted EPS was $1.23 vs a $0.76 company forecast. The fee was booked in "interest and other income" ([ValueTheMarkets](https://www.valuethemarkets.com/news/netflix-q1-earnings-surge-16-with-1225b-revenue); [Yeni Safak](https://en.yenisafak.com/technology/netflix-q1-net-income-surges-827-to-528-billion-on-28-billion-warner-bros-termination-fee-3717200)). Any trailing or CY26 P/E should exclude about $0.5/share (calc). Use CY27 EPS for the forward multiple.
3. **The "half its 10-year median P/FCF" comparison should go.** Netflix's FCF was negative or tiny in 2016–2020, so its 10-year median multiple of 57.8x means nothing.
4. **Growth is slowing despite pricing.**
   - Netflix raised US prices on **all plans in late March 2026** ([exchange4media, 2026-03-27](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/netflix-hikes-prices-on-all-plans-153318.html)), yet the Q3 guide implies about **11.7%** growth, down from 13.4% ([Hudson Labs](https://www.hudson-labs.com/research/netflix-q3-2026-earnings-preview-nflx-revenue-guidance)).
   - Total view hours were up only about 2% in H1, with per-member hours down.
   - From 2027, "What We Watched" will be published only once a year, so there will be *less* engagement disclosure.
   - Q2 buybacks of $4.7B ran about 3x Q2 FCF of $1.5B.
5. **The 2022 analogy is weak.** The 2022 recovery came from two new levers: the paid-sharing crackdown and the launch of the ad tier. Both have now been used. At about 19–20x CY27 EPS for an 11–12% grower, Netflix is fairly priced, not deeply below fundamentals.
6. **52w high.** Use $124.86 (Oct 21, 2025). The $126.71 figure is probably outside the 52-week window by Oct 9 ([Fundlibrary](https://www.fundlibrary.com/Stocks/Detail/netflix-inc/201309) showed the range $65.08–126.71 on Jul 28). Note that the $124.86 high itself rolls off after Oct 21, so "% off high" will shrink without the price moving.

**Verdict:** a smaller position (8% or less), at most one-third before Oct 20, the rest after the print or the FY27 guide in January. Cut conviction to 3/5.
