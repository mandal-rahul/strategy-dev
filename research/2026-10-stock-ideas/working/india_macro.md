## 0. Executive summary

India is in a **war-driven oil shock with a tightening cycle on top**, while domestic growth remains robust.

- **The shock.** The US/Israel–Iran war began on 28 Feb 2026. A ceasefire ran from Apr to Jul and collapsed on 8 Jul. As a result:
  - Brent is about **$102–106** (9 Oct).
  - The rupee is at **96.73/USD**, near its record low of 96.96 set in May 2026.
  - RBI **hiked the repo rate 25 bp to 5.50% on 7 Oct 2026**, its first hike since Feb 2023. It moved to a "calibrated tightening" stance and signalled that only a hike or a pause lies ahead.
  - The 10-year G-sec yield is **7.28%**, up about 75 bp year on year.
  - The monsoon ended about **12.6% deficient**, the worst since 2015, with El Niño expected to persist.
- **Growth is still strong.** Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026) real GDP grew **7.8%** and nominal GDP **10.3%**. Gross fixed capital formation grew 11.9%, bank loans are up **about 19% y/y**, and central capex is up **18% y/y** for Apr–Aug.
- **The market has corrected.** The Nifty 50 closed at **22,520 on 9 Oct**:
  - −13.8% year to date and −14.6% from its all-time high of 26,373 (5 Jan 2026).
  - The Sensex is −15% year to date.
  - Bank Nifty has outperformed at about −7% year to date. Nifty IT is about −25% year to date.
- **Valuations are now below long-run averages.**
  - Trailing P/E is about 19x, against a 5-year median of about 22x and a 10-year average of about 23x.
  - 12-month forward P/E is about **16.5–17x**, against a long-period average of about 20.5–21x.
- **Positioning is washed out among foreigners, but domestic money is still strong.**
  - FPIs have sold a record **₹2.70 lakh crore** of Indian equities in 2026 through 1 Oct, already well above the full-year 2025 total of ₹1.66 lakh crore.
  - DIIs have bought a record amount (₹5.13 trn by 7 Aug alone), and monthly SIP flows are at a record of about ₹32,300 crore.
  - Equity supply is very heavy. September 2026 was the busiest IPO month since 1996, and the **Jio Platforms IPO (about ₹30,200 crore) runs 21–23 Oct**.
- **View:** This is a **reasonable time to start buying large caps in tranches. It is not an all-clear.**
  - The swing factor is the war and oil. Trump said on 8 Oct there would be no further US military action until after the **3 Nov US midterms**.
  - The best risk/reward is in quality private banks/financials, in USD earners (IT services and pharma), which get a tailwind from the rupee at about 97 and are cheap on their own history, and in selected domestic cyclicals that are not priced for perfection.
  - Avoid crowded capex and defence names on stretched multiples, crude-sensitive sectors if you are not taking a peace-deal bet, rural/mass consumption (monsoon), and small caps given heavy supply.

### 1.4 Monsoon and agriculture: an important negative

- The 2026 southwest monsoon delivered about **759 mm, roughly 12.6% below the long-period average** (some sources say about 13%). That is "deficient" and the worst season since 2015. El Niño was the main driver. [M] ([TechTimes 30 Sep](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328334/20260930/india-monsoon-2026-ends-worst-deficit-over-decade-imd-confirms.htm), [edpublica](https://edpublica.com/india-driest-monsoon-2026-rainfall-deficit/), [DownToEarth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/south-west-monsoon-rainfall-to-be-below-normal-or-deficient-in-2026))
- Kharif sowing was 110.8 m ha as of 25 Sep, −1.2% y/y; rice acreage was −3.6%. Drought conditions in five states; the government has cut its FY27 foodgrain target. [M] ([Tridge](https://insights.tridge.com/speaker-product-news-reports/KJLyFXpzJzgKuB1Gqsm5QUKHGgR43AVdqLWkqwqhg3mpU7CPPmMTcbxn), [USDA FAS Aug-26](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/gain/2026/08/india-monsoon-recovery-fails-boost-sluggish-crop-planting))
- NOAA's September outlook puts a strong El Niño as very likely to persist through winter 2026–27, which is a risk to the rabi crop and food prices. [M]
- **Implications:** rural demand (FMCG, two-wheelers, tractors, rural NBFCs) is at risk, and food inflation is likely to rise in H2 FY27.

### 6.2 Sectors that look expensive or vulnerable

- **Defence.** The re-rating continued through the sell-off (BEL forward P/E about 41x per Kotak Neo). Order flow is real but priced in.
- **Capex and infrastructure contractors tied to central spending.** H2 FY27 fiscal squeeze: tax revenue only +3.5% and ICRA sees a ₹1.3–1.4 trn deficit overshoot.
- **Rural and mass consumption / FMCG.** The worst monsoon since 2015, El Niño, and food inflation heading to about 6%. HUL is at a 52-week low but still at about 44x forward (sibling notes).
- **Small caps broadly.** Heavy IPO supply, retail crowding, and the earnings-downgrade cycle.

- **Crude-sensitive users (OMCs, chemicals, paints, aviation, tyres)** unless you are explicitly betting on a peace deal.

### 6.3 Top five risks for Indian equities

1. **The war escalates after the 3 Nov midterms or Hormuz is disrupted again.** Brent could move towards $120 or more (dated Brent was already $125 on 6 Oct). The rupee would break its 96.96 record low, the RBI would hike again in December, and multiples would compress further.
2. **Balance of payments and the rupee.** Reserves fell $51 bn in four weeks. The FCNR-window money is already leaving (more than a third gone), and the RBI forward book is large (reportedly more than $110 bn, low confidence). A disorderly rupee move would trigger more FPI selling.
3. **Inflation and policy tightening.** CPI is projected at 6% in Q3; the monsoon was deficient with a persistent El Niño; the RBI has signalled only a hike or a pause.
4. **Earnings downgrades and fiscal slippage.** Consensus FY27 growth of about 16% looks too high (BofA was at 8.5%). A deficit overshoot could force H2 capex cuts, hurting the capex theme the market still prices richly.
5. **Flows and supply.** FPIs have sold a record ₹2.7 lakh crore and the AI-driven rotation to Taiwan and Korea persists. Meanwhile there is record issuance: September was the busiest month since 1996, Jio raises about ₹30,200 crore on 21–23 Oct, and 200+ IPOs are in the pipeline. The market depends on domestic SIP and DII flows; any slowdown in SIPs would remove the marginal buyer.

Also on the watch list:
- The US Section 301 tariff litigation and the unsigned trade deal
- AI disruption to IT
- GDP data-quality questions (the negative deflator)

### 6.4 Is now a reasonable time to buy Indian equities?

**Arguments for:**
- The Nifty is −14.6% from its all-time high, with an eight-week losing streak just broken.
- Valuations are about 16.5–17x forward, roughly 18–20% below the long-period average. Trailing P/E is about 19x against a 10-year average of about 23x.
- Foreigners are positioned light: record outflows, 14-year-low ownership and an underweight MSCI EM weight. Any oil or peace relief could trigger short covering and FPI re-entry.
- Domestic growth is strong: 7.8% real and 10.3% nominal GDP, 19% credit growth, and capex +18%.
- Domestic flows are structurally strong, with record SIPs.
- The rupee is now cheap, which helps exporters, and the US tariff disadvantage has narrowed to about 10%.
- There is a binary catalyst: Trump says the war ends "very soon".

**Arguments against:**
- The oil shock is unresolved and the war could escalate after 3 Nov.
- The RBI has just begun a tightening cycle, with CPI heading to 6%.
- The rupee is near its record low and reserves are falling fast.
- Consensus earnings look too high and downgrades are likely.
- The monsoon was deficient with El Niño persisting.
- The fiscal position is slipping.
- Heavy IPO supply is absorbing liquidity, including Jio in late October.
- India no longer offers an AI play for global EM money.
- Domestic retail is still crowded in small caps, so there has been no full capitulation.

**Bottom line (medium confidence):**
- **Start accumulating high-quality large caps in two or three tranches over October to December.** Keep dry powder for the 3 Nov to December window: the midterms, the December MPC, and absorption of the Jio IPO.
- **Tilt towards:** private banks and financials, IT and pharma exporters (rupee tailwind, cheap on their own history), and selected export manufacturers.
- **Underweight:** small caps, defence and capex contractors on stretched multiples, rural consumption, and rate-sensitive sectors.
- A credible ceasefire or a Hormuz reopening (Brent below $85) would be the signal to add cyclicals and crude users aggressively.
- Brent sustained above $115 together with the rupee above 97 would be the signal to slow buying.
